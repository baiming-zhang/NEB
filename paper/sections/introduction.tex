\section{Introduction}
\begingroup
Repeated flow queries arise in interactive simulation, initial-condition exploration, and geometric design. Reduced-order models (ROMs) make these queries affordable by evolving a small set of spatial coordinates. The basis determines which motions the reduced state can express, how physical constraints enter the model, and which computations can be reused. A useful fluid basis should therefore connect spatial structure to dynamics: its coordinates should remain physically interpretable as they are evaluated at new locations and combined into new initial states. We pursue this connection through a continuous neural representation of a fluid spectrum.

Fluid model reduction has developed along both data-driven and operator-driven routes. Snapshot-based methods extract a compact span from simulated flows: \citet{treuille2006model} demonstrated interactive reduced fluid dynamics, including coupling to moving obstacles, and \citet{kim2013subspace} combined subspace integration and cubature to re-simulate variations of a high-resolution sequence. These methods exploit the structure of a flow collection to reduce spatial evaluation and recurrent computation. Spectral methods instead define coordinates through a differential operator. Laplacian Eigenfluids \citep{dewitt2012fluid} associate spatial scales with eigenvalues and give viscous decay a modal interpretation; model-reduced variational simulation connects this construction to geometric structure and harmonic fields \citep{liu2015model}. An operator-defined span is organized by differential energy independently of a chosen trajectory. Its fluid constraints and circulation coordinates provide a natural structure to preserve in a learned representation.

Neural fluid models provide several complementary forms of reuse. Deep Fluids learns a parameterized velocity generator and latent evolution from simulation data \citep{kim2019deep}. DeepONet and Fourier Neural Operators learn mappings between function spaces \citep{lu2021deeponet,li2021fourier}, while Geo-FNO incorporates learned domain deformations for general geometries \citep{li2023geometry}. These approaches learn how a family of physical inputs maps to flow solutions. Learning spatial coordinates supplies another reusable object: once a geometry-specific basis and its operators are prepared, initial conditions can share them through an explicit coefficient state. This separation makes the quality of the spatial representation and the accuracy of the reduced dynamics individually measurable.

Coordinate-based neural fields supply continuous spatial values and derivatives. Periodic activations support differentiable signals \citep{sitzmann2020implicit}, and Fourier features improve high-frequency representation \citep{tancik2020fourier}. Variational learning then chooses fields according to an operator: the Deep Ritz method minimizes physical energies \citep{e2018deep}, Spectral Inference Networks learn multiple eigenfunctions through stochastic optimization \citep{pfau2019spectral}, and neural Rayleigh methods address engineering eigenproblems \citep{rowan2025rayleigh}. Shape Space Spectra extends variational eigenfunction learning to parameterized shapes, using geometry-local reordering and causal orthogonalization to handle changing spectral order \citep{chang2025shapespacespectra}. Fluid eigenfields bring additional structure to this problem. The trial space must express incompressibility, wall impermeability, equivalent potential representatives, and the independent circulation allowed by topology.

Physical constraints can enter the neural representation directly. Neural Conservation Laws (NCL) constructs divergence-free fields through differential lifts of antisymmetric potentials \citep{richterpowell2022neural}; constrained neural basis methods likewise use divergence-free trial spaces for incompressible problems \citep{cheng2026decoupled}. Neural Kinematic Bases (NKB) learns direct-velocity coordinates with losses for smoothness, divergence, wall alignment, and orthogonality, supporting mesh-free flow fitting and animation \citep{liu2025neural}. We combine differential admissibility with operator-based spectral selection. A scalar streamfunction in two dimensions or a vector potential in three generates divergence-free velocity, its boundary trace imposes impermeability, and geometry-local orthogonalization separates the coordinates. Rayleigh minimization orders these admissible fields without flow snapshots in the spectral loss. The NKB experiments provide a matched-budget architecture comparison within the same Galerkin assessment.

Potential-space learning offers a lower-derivative route to these coordinates when its gauge is selected consistently. Constant streamfunctions and admissible curl-free vector fields leave velocity unchanged while changing the potential mass norm. Selecting a representative makes normalization and energy minimization well defined. On compatible canonical spaces and boundary domains, the classical differential lift pairs the exact positive potential and velocity spectra \citep{arnold2006finite}. We derive the spectral-variance difference between their Rayleigh objectives for approximate modes. This relation links finite-capacity accuracy to the objective's weighting of spectral content. The auxiliary-solver study connects differentiable representative selection to source-space resolution and operator consistency.

Topology supplies an independent circulation sector. Hodge theory identifies harmonic velocities that carry circulation around obstacles \citep{arnold2006finite}. Their physical role is developed by spectral variational ROMs \citep{liu2015model}, Fluid Cohomology \citep{yin2023fluid}, and Hodge-based neural operator learning \citep{zheng2026hodge}. We learn harmonic measures using fixed boundary traces and a Dirichlet energy, then lift them to velocity fields. Positive modes and harmonic coordinates together express vortical motion and independent obstacle circulation. Their allocation within a fixed total dimension exposes a practical distinction: harmonic completion makes the represented space topologically complete, while its energy benefit depends on how strongly the particular flow occupies that sector.

\begin{samepage}
Our contributions are threefold:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt,topsep=4pt]
\item \textbf{Physically constrained neural eigenfluid coordinates.} A continuous geometry-conditioned representation combines differential lifts, impermeable traces, and geometry-local spectral orthogonalization. Geometry-specific Galerkin operators support autonomous coefficient dynamics and spatial resampling.
\item \textbf{Potential-space spectral learning with gauge selection.} We formulate learning on canonical potential representatives, apply the classical compatible positive-spectrum pairing, and derive the spectral-variance difference between potential and velocity losses.
\item \textbf{Neural circulation coordinates.} A boundary-constrained harmonic loss supplies the independent topological sector. Fixed-budget allocation and matched lift-conditioning comparisons connect these coordinates to energy capture and flow evolution.
\end{enumerate}
\end{samepage}
